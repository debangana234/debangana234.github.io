Abstract

Peripheral blood DNA methylation is a promising source of molecular biomarkers for Parkinson's disease, but it remains unclear whether disease-associated methylation alterations are already detectable during the prodromal phase. This project used longitudinal whole-blood Illumina EPIC methylation profiles from the TREND cohort to study methylation and immune-composition changes across healthy controls, prodromal converters, and clinically diagnosed Parkinson's disease patients.

The analysis combined reference-based cell-type deconvolution, epigenetic age acceleration, differential methylation analysis, DMR analysis, functional enrichment, and validation in public Parkinson's disease methylation datasets. The results link immune-composition shifts, epigenetic variation, and disease progression, supporting peripheral blood DNA methylation as a potential marker of early Parkinson's disease-associated molecular alterations.

Study design

Research Questions

Cell Type Proportions

  • How does the cell-type proportion vary in Parkinson's disease compared to controls?
  • How do cell-type proportions change in diagnosed individuals compared to individuals in the pre-clinical stage of the disease?
  • Are the observations coherent with findings from other literature?

Epigenetic Age

  • How does epigenetic age versus chronological age vary in Parkinson's disease patients with respect to controls?
  • How does epigenetic age acceleration vary between the individual groups?
  • What is the correlation of epigenetic age acceleration with disease status and cell-type proportions?

Differential Methylation

  • How does methylation at individual CpG sites vary between the individual groups?
  • Are there groups of CpG sites that show a patterned methylation difference between the individual groups?
  • How does methylation vary in significant CpG sites in public datasets and other studies?
  • Can a set of differentially methylated CpG sites from TREND predict disease status with fair accuracy in other datasets?

Datasets

The dataset was organized around the idea that Parkinson's disease develops over time, with molecular changes potentially detectable in blood before clinical diagnosis.

The primary TREND dataset followed participants across different disease-related stages. Controls were represented at early, mid, and late time points, while converters contributed samples before and after conversion to Parkinson's disease. This structure made it possible to compare stable control trajectories with individuals moving through a prodromal phase into clinically defined disease.

The longitudinal staging was important for the thesis because blood DNA methylation can capture both disease-associated epigenetic alterations and changes in immune-cell composition. By separating late controls, pre-converters, and post-converters, we could ask whether cell composition and methylation patterns already shift before diagnosis.

Reference deconvolution

The deconvolution step used the EPIC reference dataset, which provides methylation signatures from purified blood-cell types, to estimate cell-type proportions in each whole-blood sample.

Longitudinal composition

  • Immune-cell proportions were compared as converters moved from the pre-diagnosis period to the post-diagnosis period.
  • Samples were aligned to the diagnosis date because participants were diagnosed at different ages.
  • The analysis focused on whether monocytes, NK cells, and neutrophils changed consistently around conversion.

Key takeaways

  1. The cellular-composition pattern does not appear to follow a simple linear stage order; instead, it suggests a rise toward the pre-converter stage followed by a decrease after diagnosis.
    Early control < Late control < Pre-converter > Post-converter
  2. Mixed models were fitted with age, sex, and a subject-specific random intercept to account for repeated measurements from the same TREND participants.
  3. The categorical stage effect remained significant for neutrophils, lymphocyte proportion, and the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio.
  4. The strongest pattern appeared around the pre-converter stage, where neutrophils increased while total lymphocyte proportion decreased.
  5. Post-diagnosis samples should be interpreted cautiously because treatment after diagnosis could also contribute to inflammatory changes.

Epigenetic age analysis

The Horvath clock was used to estimate DNA methylation age and calculate epigenetic age acceleration as the residual variation after accounting for chronological age.

The analysis tested whether age acceleration and immune-cell composition varied with disease stage and whether these variables explained structured differences between samples.

Key takeaways

Clock performance The Horvath clock explained less age-prediction variation in converter or Parkinson's disease samples than in controls, suggesting that converter biology may contain nonlinear disease-associated variation not captured by chronological age alone.
Stage effect Disease stage alone did not fully explain epigenetic age acceleration, so EAA should not be interpreted as a simple stage-only signal.
Cell composition The PCA biplot suggests that EAA varies together with immune-cell composition; increased neutrophil contribution aligns with increased Horvath EAA.
Key message Epigenetic age acceleration appears to reflect an interplay between disease stage and immune-cell composition rather than a single isolated disease-stage effect.

The heatmap on the left represents the average methylation across all CpGs associated with each DMR and is labelled by the gene associated with that region. The scrollable zoom panel shows individual methylation levels at neighbouring CpGs within selected DMRs, using the LDHC-associated region as a hypomethylated example and the ACAP1-associated region as a hypermethylated example.

Key takeaways

  1. The zoomed CpGs include CNGA1, which is associated with retinitis pigmentosa and has been reported in a PD context through co-occurrence with an SNCAIP mutation, and LDHC, whose paralog LDHA has been linked to metabolic alterations in idiopathic PD.
  2. The PTPRN2-linked CpG is biologically relevant because PTPRN2 has been associated with PD in previous studies, including reports of altered methylation in PD brain tissue.
  3. The same CpG sites appear more uniform in the Horvath and Vallerga datasets, suggesting that the TREND-specific DMP signal is not strongly replicated, although beta-value distributions remain broadly comparable.
  4. DMR-level analysis showed that methylation changes were coordinated across CpGs for 11 regions, which provides more interpretability than isolated DMPs.

Combined-Rank CpGs

The genes from the 12 DMRs did not provide enrichment. Therefore, to increase the gene set, additional CpG sites were selected using CombinedRank, a rank assigned to each CpG site based on both adjusted p-values and logFC values. The top 500 CpGs selected by this rank were used for Gene Ontology enrichment using g:Profiler.

Predictive Modelling in Unseen Datasets

Predictive modelling was used to test whether methylation signatures could classify Parkinson's disease status in independent public methylation datasets. Logistic regression, SVM with linear kernel, and SVM with radial kernel were compared across 100 to 400 CpG sites; the best follow-up model combined 200 highly variable CpGs with estimated cell-type proportions.

Summary

Innate immune-cell shifts were prominent around the prodromal and pre-converter phase.

Higher CD4+ and CD8+ T-cell proportions were linked to lower epigenetic age acceleration, while higher neutrophil proportions correlated with higher EAA, suggesting an immune-aging axis in PD.

The late-control versus post-converter comparison identified 91 differentially methylated positions in TREND.

Region-level methylation patterns supported a structured epigenetic transition across TREND stages.

Although exact replication across datasets was limited, beta-value distributions across TREND DMPs were similar in the Horvath and Vallerga datasets, suggesting possible underlying PD-related mechanisms.

GABAergic signalling enrichment suggests that selected CpG sites may be functionally relevant, but larger cohorts are required for robust biomarker validation.
